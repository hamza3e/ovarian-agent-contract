    How I used this guide

    I asked Claude Code to write this guide on 19 November 2026, while the repository was being built, because the first background notes were hard for me to follow. I then worked through it step by step, and it is how I came to understand the project.

    What I did with it

    I read sections 2 to 6 (reports, grade, FIGO staging, residual disease) before labelling anything. I then labelled 5 development practice reports and 2 test-centre reports, blind to model outputs, at 7 to 11 minutes of active time per practice report (D26, D27). A field-by-field review of my practice labels against the annotation guide (by Claude Code, development reports only) disagreed on 16 of 70 fields, mostly lymphovascular invasion, uterus involvement, omental deposit size and histotype. My conclusion was that a gold set for these fields needs a pathologist, so I dropped it for this version (D29).

    How to read it

    Sections 1 to 6 are the medical background (about 45 minutes), 7 to 10 the ML and LLM ideas (about 40 minutes), 11 the system itself, 12 a short self-test. Each idea comes as a definition, an everyday analogy, and what it means in this project. "Mark as read" is saved only in your browser.

    1. The big picture

    Multi-agent AI systems for cancer care, such as the EU IMPACT-OC project for ovarian cancer, combine separate agents for imaging, pathology, genomics and clinical text.

    In one sentence

    Specialist "agents" for imaging, pathology, genetics and clinical text, and a "super-agent" that combines their answers. The aim is recommendations for the tumour board (the clinicians' case meeting) that state their uncertainty openly. Pilots of such systems run in "shadow mode": in the background, without affecting treatment.

    In a hospital the radiologist, the pathologist and the geneticist each write a report, and the oncologist combines them at the tumour board. The agents help the specialists; the super-agent helps the board.

    Where the problem is

    To combine agents, each one has to speak the same language: the answer, the evidence, how sure it is, and the ability to say "I don't know". This repository tries that layer on one agent, the pathology report agent, with public data.

    2. Ovarian cancer: the minimum

    Enough to talk with clinicians about the data: not expert knowledge, but the right words.

    HGSC: the main disease

    • Most ovarian cancers are epithelial. The most common and most lethal subtype is high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC).
    • Most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage (III or IV), because early symptoms are few.
    • Many HGSCs are thought to start in the fallopian tube (precursor lesion: STIC, serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma). Reports therefore say "tubo-ovarian", and the site of origin can be unclear.
    • Genetics: in the TCGA study (Nature 2011) almost all HGSCs had a TP53 mutation (about 96%), and about half had homologous recombination deficiency (HRD, including BRCA1/2).

    Treatment, roughly

    • Surgery (debulking / cytoreduction): remove all visible tumour, aiming to leave no macroscopic disease.
    • Platinum-based chemotherapy: the standard drug treatment.
    • PARP inhibitor: maintenance treatment when there is a BRCA mutation or HRD.
    • The main decision: surgery first (PDS, primary debulking surgery), or chemotherapy first and surgery later (NACT + IDS, neoadjuvant chemotherapy and interval debulking surgery)?
    • CA-125: a blood tumour marker used in follow-up. CRS (chemotherapy response score): the pathological grade (1 to 3) of the omentum's response to chemotherapy after NACT.
    Before putting out a fire you either go straight in and put it all out (PDS), or shrink it from outside first and then go in (NACT + IDS). The right choice depends on the guess "can I put all of it out once I am inside?"
    The reports come from TCGA, so most are primary surgery specimens. The agent does not make this decision; it extracts the findings that would feed into it, reliably.

    3. How to read a pathology report

    The report below is synthetic (written for the tests, not a real patient). Click a field to see which part of the report supports it.

    • A report is made of specimen parts: A right ovary, B left ovary, C omentum, D uterus, E lymph nodes...
    • Each part has a gross description (size, appearance) and a microscopic final diagnosis. Some reports end with a structured synoptic report.
    • Frozen section: a quick diagnosis during surgery. Washings / cytology: fluid from the abdominal cavity, often reported separately.
    • The real TCGA reports were OCR'd from scanned paper, so typos such as "lort ovary" or "Quentum" are normal.
    Pick a field.
    Why is this hard?Laterality is spread over two parts: A "Ovary, right" and B "Ovary, left" together mean "bilateral". A regex that reads sentence by sentence cannot join them, and on the development centres the rules baseline was weakest exactly here. This is where the LLM is expected to help (D21).
    This report has no "residual disease" information, so the correct answer is not_stated. "The report does not say" is also an answer.

    4. Histotype and grade

    The two fields that come up most often when labelling.

    Old system: three tiers

    G1 well differentiated, G2 moderately, G3 poorly. TCGA-era reports and the registry use this.

    Current WHO: two separate diseases

    LGSC (low-grade serous): KRAS/BRAF pathway, slow course. HGSC (high-grade serous): TP53, genomic instability, sensitive to platinum and PARP inhibitors. They are not two grades of the same disease.

    Labelling rules (from the annotation guide)

    • Grade is what the report says: "poorly differentiated" = G3. Do not turn G2 into "high grade" automatically.
    • If several grades are given, take the highest.
    • If there is no three-tier grade and the report says "high grade", use high_grade.
    • Histotype: "serous carcinoma, poorly differentiated" → serous_nos (the grade goes to its own field). Use high_grade_serous only when the words "high grade serous" appear.
    Registry (silver label) grades for the 371 patients: G3 313, G2 42, G1 6, missing 10. The registry is not the truth. Results are reported as agreement with it, never as accuracy (D29).

    5. FIGO staging, with a calculator

    Stage says how far the disease has spread. Staging is rule-based, so here it is computed by rules rather than guessed by the LLM.

    Which version did TCGA use?The registry contains stage IIC (16 of the 371 patients). IIC was removed in the 2014 version, and the diagnosis years are mostly 1999 to 2009. So TCGA used FIGO 1988. Comparing against the wrong version would have shown "errors" even for a correct system.
    StageRoughly1988 vs 2014
    IConfined to the ovary or ovaries. IA one ovary, IB both, IC surface tumour / capsule rupture / tumour in washings2014 splits IC into IC1 (surgical spill), IC2 (surface / rupture), IC3 (cytology)
    IIPelvic extension. IIA uterus / tube, IIB other pelvic tissues1988's IIC (II plus surface / cytology) was removed in 2014
    IIIPeritoneum outside the pelvis (including omentum) and/or retroperitoneal lymph nodesNodes only: 1988 IIIC, 2014 IIIA1. Microscopic peritoneal disease: 1988 IIIA, 2014 IIIA2
    IVDistant metastasis: tumour in pleural fluid, liver or spleen parenchyma, organs outside the abdomen2014 IVA (pleural) / IVB (parenchymal, extra-abdominal, including inguinal nodes)

    The omentum lies above the pelvis, so omental tumour means at least stage III: deposits ≤ 2 cm give IIIB, > 2 cm give IIIC.

    Simplifications: in 2014, stage I means "confined to the ovaries or tubes", so tube involvement is not always stage II; IC1 (surgical spill) and IIIA1(i)/(ii) (nodal metastasis ≤ / > 10 mm) are not shown. Liver capsule involvement counts as III, parenchymal as IV.

    The report agent cannot see stage IV (pleura and liver parenchyma are not in an abdominal pathology report), so it gives a lower bound such as "≥ IIIC", and imaging or a clinical agent would complete it. This is a concrete reason for a multi-agent design. A study on 390 ovarian masses (O-RADS, arXiv 2608.23061) found that "LLM extracts, rules decide" reached 99.2% accuracy, while end-to-end LLM strategies ranged from 65.6% to 95.9%. This repository tests the same idea for FIGO staging (D6).

    6. Residual disease: an honesty test

    One of the strongest prognostic factors, but rarely written in a pathology report.

    • Tumour left at the end of surgery: R0 / no macroscopic disease, ≤ 1 cm (optimal), > 1 cm (suboptimal).
    • It is a surgical finding: the surgeon sees what was left in the abdomen; the pathologist only sees the tissue that was removed. The pathology report usually does not say.
    • The registry has it for most patients, but the reports rarely mention it: the word "residual" appears in 63 of the 371 reports (a rough keyword count).
    If a match report only covers the first half, the right answer to "what was the final score?" is "not in the report", not a guess.
    A good agent says not_stated here instead of inventing a value. If a phrase such as "optimal debulking" appears and it cannot back its answer with a quote, it abstains. One synthetic test checks exactly this: an answer whose quote is not in the report makes the hybrid agent abstain, while the end-to-end agent answers with a warning.

    7. Calibration: is "90% sure" really 90%?

    A model is calibrated if it is right about 90 times out of the 100 answers it gives with 90% confidence.

    If a forecast says "70% chance of rain" and it rains on about 70% of those days, the forecaster is calibrated. One that says "95%" every day and is wrong half the time is overconfident.
    Toy data, not a result. Bars below the diagonal mean the model is overconfident: when it says "90%" it is right less often. This is a reliability diagram; the average gap between the bars and the line is the ECE (expected calibration error).

    Measures

    • ECE: the weighted average gap between stated confidence and observed accuracy, over confidence bins. Smaller is better.
    • Brier score: the mean of (confidence − outcome)². It reflects both accuracy and calibration.
    • Calibrator: a small function that maps raw confidence to observed accuracy. Common choices are Platt scaling (a logistic curve) and isotonic regression (a monotone step curve). Here it is an L2 logistic regression on several confidence features (protocol §5).
    The calibrator is fitted only on the calibration centres (Pittsburgh, MD Anderson, IGC), against registry agreement for grade, laterality and LVI, and measured on the 14 unseen test centres. This is calibration transfer: does confidence tuned at one hospital still hold at another?

    8. Abstention and risk–coverage

    An agent that says "I don't know" when unsure answers fewer questions but makes fewer mistakes on the ones it answers. That trade-off has to be measured.

    A test where wrong answers cost points: leave the questions you are unsure of blank, and you answer fewer but score higher.

    Toy data: 20 invented predictions, not a result. The number in each box is the model's confidence; green is right, red is wrong.

    • Risk–coverage curve: the pairs of coverage and risk as the threshold moves. The area under it is the AURC; smaller is better.
    • Selective prediction: single numbers such as "accuracy at 90% coverage".
    The contract keeps not_stated and abstained apart. "The report does not say" is a correct answer; "I am not sure" is a refusal. Merging them makes an agent that refuses often look good and hides missing information. For a super-agent the difference matters: not_stated means "look at another source", abstained means "ask a person".

    9. Site shift and the splits

    Every hospital has its own report template, terms, scan quality and patients. A model that works at one centre can fail at another (site shift).

    A student tested only on their own teacher's questions looks good, and may not do as well on another school's exam.

    Development

    109

    Washington Univ., Christiana, MSK. The only reports looked at while writing prompts and rules.

    Calibration

    75

    Pittsburgh, MD Anderson, IGC. Only the calibrator and thresholds are fitted here.

    Test

    187

    14 unseen centres (Imperial College among them). Locked in code until the protocol is frozen.

    • Why not a random split? A random split puts reports from the same centre in both training and test. The model learns the template, the result looks better than it is, and differences between centres disappear.
    • Test lock: oac.splits.select does not return test reports until eval/PROTOCOL.md is frozen with its hash recorded and OAC_UNLOCK_TEST=1 is set. The git history shows the order: protocol first, test run second.
    • Conformal prediction: the model returns a set ("G2 or G3") instead of one answer and aims to guarantee how often the set contains the right answer. The guarantee assumes the data are exchangeable, which can break when the centre changes. The contract has a field for prediction sets; they are not part of the current protocol.

    10. The LLM side: schema, evidence, consistency

    The LLM has a narrow job: extract findings and quote evidence. Code decides everything else.

    Constrained decoding

    vLLM turns the JSON schema into a grammar and blocks, at every step, the tokens that would break it. The model cannot produce invalid JSON or a value that is not allowed, such as "G4".

    A drop-down menu on a form: you can only pick listed options, but you can still pick the wrong one.

    It guarantees the format, not the content. Content is checked by evidence verification and, for some fields, by agreement with the registry.

    Evidence verification

    For every answer the model quotes the report. Code looks for the quote in the report: first exactly, then ignoring case and spacing, then by fuzzy matching with a threshold of 90/100. If it is not found, the hybrid agent's answer becomes abstained: evidence_not_verified.

    Known limitVerification proves the quote exists, not that it supports the answer. The model could quote "Ovary, left" and answer "bilateral". Registry agreement catches some of this for grade, laterality and LVI; a check between the value and its quote is a next step.

    Confidence signals

    SignalHow it is computedWhat it tells you
    Self-consistency1 greedy and 5 sampled outputs; how many samples agree with the greedy answer5/5 stable, 2/5 unstable. Catches ambiguous reports.
    Token probabilityThe probability the model gives to the tokens of "G3": exp(sum of log-probabilities)Local confidence; usually too high, so not used alone
    Evidence match scoreFuzzy match score of the quote against the report (0–100)Invented quote, or just an OCR difference?

    The model is not asked how confident it is: verbal confidence is known to be poorly calibrated (Kadavath et al. 2022). Signals from its behaviour go to the calibrator instead.

    Hybrid and end-to-end

    End-to-end (baseline)

    Same model; its answers are taken as they are and it assigns the stage itself. Unverified evidence only adds a warning.

    Hybrid (the proposed system)

    The model extracts; code verifies the evidence, calibrates confidence and abstains when unsure; FIGO rules compute the stage.

    Why a local open-weight model

    • Hospital data does not leave the site, which matches federated, "data stays on site" designs.
    • Everything is pinned: Qwen3-8B-AWQ, its Hugging Face revision, the prompt hash, the code commit and the seed. The same run can be repeated.
    • It runs on a single Colab GPU; the recorded runs used an NVIDIA L4.
    • An optional Claude API backend exists to measure what keeping the data in-house costs in accuracy. It is not part of the evaluation protocol.

    12. Test yourself

    Think of your own answer first, then open it.

    1. The report only says "para-aortic lymph nodes 2/5 positive"; the omentum is negative and the tumour is in one ovary. What is the FIGO 1988 and 2014 stage?
    1988: IIIC (retroperitoneal nodes alone give IIIC). 2014: IIIA1 (retroperitoneal nodes only; IIIA1(i) or (ii) by the size of the nodal metastasis). Try "One ovary" and "Pelvic / para-aortic nodes positive" in the calculator. The report agent still says "≥ IIIC", because it cannot see stage IV.
    2. Why is residual disease hard to extract from a pathology report, and what should the agent do?
    It is a surgical finding: the surgeon sees what was left behind, the pathologist only sees the removed tissue. The report usually does not say, so the correct answer is not_stated. If there is a vague phrase (for example "optimal") and the agent is not sure, it abstains. It never fills the value in from the registry or clinical data.
    3. If calibration were fitted and tested on a random split, what problem would we miss?
    Site shift. Reports from the same centre would be in both calibration and test, so calibration would look good and could still fail at a new hospital. The models will be used at other centres, so the real question is calibration transfer.
    4. With constrained decoding, why is there still an "invalid_model_output" reason?
    If the output hits max_tokens, the JSON is cut off. The schema also allows a null value under "answered", which code catches. With the Claude backend, a refusal returns empty text. Broken output is recorded, not repaired, because repairing it would hide the failure.
    5. The quoted sentence is in the report but does not support the answer. Does the evidence check catch it?
    No. Verification proves existence, not support. Registry agreement catches some of these cases for grade, laterality and LVI. The next step is a rule-based check between the value and its quote (a "left" quote contradicts a "bilateral" answer), or a second checking model.
    6. What does self-consistency add on top of token probability?
    Token probability measures local confidence along one output path. Sampling shows whether the answer changes when other paths are tried. Even if the greedy answer has a high token probability, samples that disagree mean the report is ambiguous. The two signals complement each other, and the calibrator uses both.
